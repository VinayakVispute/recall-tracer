Real Recall Incidents

Our demo company is synthetic, but the problem is not. These three real incidents show what goes wrong when a contaminated ingredient moves through other companies' products, and why a traceability graph fixes it.

Two of the three are already in our FalkorDB fda graph, loaded from openFDA enforcement reports and CDC outbreak pages. Every number on this page comes from those records or from the cited FDA and CDC pages, as of Oct 17, 2026.

Clover Hill Dairy, Listeria in requesón

A Listeria outbreak linked to requesón, a soft ricotta-style cheese, with illness onsets from March 2023 to July 2026. CDC linked it to Clover Hill Dairy, LLC of Mechanicsville, Maryland, which filed 18 Class I recalls from June 1, 2026.

14in hospital
1death
18Class I recalls by the firm
Recalls namedMDNYVA People got sick inIL · 1MD · 4NY · 6VA · 4

The gap. Someone got sick in Illinois, and no recall from this firm named Illinois. The records do not say how the cheese got there.

Two other firms also recalled requesón in the same weeks: Nelson & Isa Lacteos LLC (recall H-1189-2026) and La Ceiba Foods Latin Market Inc. Our graph links them to Clover Hill as inferred suppliers, because CDC's updates list them together but no record states the shipment.

The query that finds the gap

MATCH (o:Outbreak)-[:CASES_IN]->(s:State), (o)-[:LINKED_TO]->(f:Firm)
WHERE NOT (f)-[:RECALLED]->(:Recall)-[:DISTRIBUTED_TO]->(s)
RETURN o.id AS outbreak, s.code AS state

Already built: the real-data page shows this outbreak's record network and runs this query live, in under a millisecond of FalkorDB time.

California Dairies, Salmonella in milk powder

On April 20, 2026, California Dairies Inc. of Visalia, California, recalled non-fat dried milk powder and buttermilk powder, sold in 2,200 lb totes and 25 kg bags, for possible Salmonella. Milk powder is an ingredient, so the recall did not stop there. Over the next 16 days, seven other companies recalled their own products because they were made with it.

8companies recalled
22Class I product recalls
16 daysfrom the first recall to the last
42states named, plus one nationwide recall

The source didn't know where it went. California Dairies' own recall lists its US distribution as "Unknown and to be determined during RAC assignment". Its customers picked the powder up from the plant, and it also went to Mexico, the Philippines and the Dominican Republic.

What this shows

  • The source can't scope its own recall. California Dairies didn't know where its powder went in the US. Each customer had to find out, then recall on its own schedule, over 16 days.
  • The name on the pack isn't the company that recalled it. A shopper holding Kroger croutons or Giant Eagle pita chips would not know to look for "California Dairies".
  • It goes two and three steps deep. Milk powder went into seasonings sold to popcorn and grocery brands, into croutons and pita chips sold under store names, and into sauce mixes sold under Aramark's name.
  • This is our product's trace on real records. Lot, into batches, into products, out to customers. Recall Tracer does this for one company's own records in about 25 seconds, and proves which stores are clear.

Say it carefully. Only three recalls name California Dairies. The other four say "recalled by the supplier", so their links are inferred from the same ingredient in the same weeks. No illnesses are reported for this cascade, so don't claim any.

McDonald's onions, E. coli O157:H7

In October 2024, sick people across 14 states had eaten Quarter Pounder burgers at McDonald's. Investigators had to work out which ingredient in the same burger was contaminated: the fresh slivered onions or the beef patties. Traceback pointed to yellow onions from Taylor Farms, which recalled them on October 22, 2024.

104people sick
14states
34in hospital
63 of 75who remembered their order ate slivered onions

Why it fits our story. "Onions or beef?" is the question our outbreak page answers: which ingredient lot explains every case. Our demo ranks L-204 above L-162 and rules out a decoy, using the graph instead of weeks of interviews and traceback.

This outbreak is from 2024, before our openFDA data starts, so it isn't in the graph. Use it as a spoken line, not a screen.

// The cascade, after the SUPPLIED links are loaded
MATCH (src:Firm {name: 'California Dairies Inc.'})-[s:SUPPLIED]->(f:Firm)-[:RECALLED]->(r:Recall)
OPTIONAL MATCH (r)-[:DISTRIBUTED_TO]->(st:State)
RETURN f.name AS firm, s.basis AS basis, count(DISTINCT r) AS recalls,
       min(r.initiated) AS first_recall, collect(DISTINCT st.code) AS states
ORDER BY first_recall
