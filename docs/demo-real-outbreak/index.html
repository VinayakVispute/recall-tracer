Sprout Outbreak Demo Plan

Open the demo on an outbreak that is still under investigation. Upload the case report CDC published. The graph finds the one lot code that three companies' recalls share, and shows which states with sick people the first recall of that lot never named.

Why this outbreak

  • It is live. CDC and FDA last updated it on October 1, 2026. Judges can open the government pages themselves.
  • It is our product's story in real life. One seed lot, SAL2-24JB, went from a seed supplier to sprout growers, wholesale distributors and two retail seed repackers. People got sick in 16 states.
  • The data is in our graph. The openFDA snapshot holds the seed supplier's and one repacker's recalls, both naming SAL2-24JB. The second repacker's recall is an FDA notice not yet in openFDA; we load it from the notice, with its link.
  • It has a real gap. The seed supplier's recall named 6 of the 16 states with cases. The other 10 were reached only by retail seed packets, recalled 19 and 31 days later.
  • It replaces Clover Hill as the opener. Clover Hill showed one missing state. This shows a lot code joining three companies and ten states the first recall missed.

One lot, three companies

Public recall records are filed company by company. Nothing in openFDA says these recalls belong together. The graph does it in one step: read the lot code from each recall's code text, make the lot a node, and every recall that names it hangs off that node. Of 281 lot codes in 2,647 recalls, SAL2-24JB is the only one named by more than one company.

Solid green: the openFDA recall record names lot SAL2-24JB. Dashed blue: the FDA recall notice names it; openFDA doesn't carry it yet.

// FalkorDB: every recall that names the same lot code, across companies
MATCH (f:Firm)-[:RECALLED]->(r:Recall)-[:NAMES_LOT]->(l:LotCode {code: 'SAL2-24JB'})
RETURN f.name, r.id, r.initiated, r.distribution ORDER BY r.initiated

The gap the graph finds

The 16 states with cases, by the recall of the lot that first named them:

The finding. Ten of the sixteen states with sick people are not on the seed supplier's shipping list. The lot reached them only through retail seed repackers, whose recalls came 19 and 31 days after the supplier's. The sprout growers who got the lot aren't named in public records at all.

This is the honest version of the product's pitch: public records stop at the company. Inside a company, FSMA 204 lot records let the seed supplier list every customer of SAL2-24JB, and each grower list every store, in one query. That is what Recall Tracer does on our demo company.

The upload moment

CDC doesn't publish individual case reports; it publishes the states, the case total and the onset window. The demo uploads exactly that, as a CSV anyone could build from CDC's page. The file is in the repo at web/public/samples/cdc-alfalfa-sprouts-2026.csv and downloadable from the card.

On the outbreak page, the Public case report card, about a second after the upload and with no agent call:

  1. The haystack. 83 recalls from 28 firms name these pathogens since the first illness. Six firms' recalls reach all 16 case states, so where a recall went can't single out a source.
  2. The lot. 10 lot codes in their records; one, SAL2-24JB, is named by three different firms. The chain draws: case report, lot, and the three recalls.
  3. The coverage. The first recall of the lot named 6 of 16 case states; the other 10 only by later recalls, up to 19 days later.
  4. The timeline. First illness May 31 · seed supplier Aug 23 · Todd's Sep 11 · Berlin Sep 23. 84 days to the first recall of the lot, 31 more until the last.
  5. The queries. Two FalkorDB queries, about 20 to 50 ms, shown under the result.

What we can and can't claim

Can say. Three companies' recalls name lot SAL2-24JB: two in openFDA, one in an FDA recall notice. The seed supplier's distribution list covers 6 of the 16 case states; the repackers' recalls cover the rest. The dates and counts are as published.

Can't say.

  • That Recall Tracer would have found the source earlier. The recall records we join only exist because the investigation produced them.
  • That a case in a given state came from a given company. CDC publishes states, not case-level purchases.
  • That Everything Sprouts used lot SAL2-24JB. FDA lists its recall in the advisory, but no public record names the seed lot it used.
  • That FDA named International Sprout Holdings. FDA's advisory does not; the openFDA recall record carries the lot code.
  • Hospitalization or death counts other than FDA's: 6 hospitalized, no deaths, as of October 1, 2026.

Say it once in the video: "The outbreak and recalls are real public records. The company in the rest of the demo is a demo company."

Backups

  • The upload fails on the day. Run the two queries from "Show the queries" in FalkorDB Browser instead.
  • CDC or FDA updates the numbers before recording. Re-scrape the two pages and the openFDA data the morning of the recording; the script's numbers come from those, not from this page.
  • openFDA is unreachable. The snapshot in data/real already holds everything the opener needs.
  • Clover Hill remains a fallback opener. It is loaded and working today.
